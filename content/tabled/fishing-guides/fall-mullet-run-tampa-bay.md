**Quick answer:** The fall mullet run on Tampa Bay runs from October into December, peaking in November, and it switches on four of the bay's biggest predators at once: snook, redfish, tarpon and sharks. Huge schools of mullet move through the bay toward the Gulf, and the fish that eat them follow.

## What the mullet run is

Every fall, striped mullet gather into big schools and leave the backwaters and shallow bays for the Gulf, where they spawn. You see it on the surface first: dark patches of fish pushing along the shorelines, flipping and jumping, sometimes so thick the water looks like it's boiling.

The timing follows water temperature and shorter days, so it shifts a little each year. The first real cool spell of fall usually gets the schools moving.

## Why snook and redfish turn on

Snook hold along mangrove shorelines, docks and passes, waiting for mullet to swim past, and fall is when snook work back into the bay from the beaches. Redfish feed along the flats, oyster bars and mangrove tree lines, right where our October trips found them.

## Why tarpon and sharks show up too

Tarpon peak from May through August, but some stay into fall and feed right in the middle of the schools. Sharks are even more reliable, shadowing the schools along the passes and channels and picking off mullet at the edges.

## How a mullet run trip plays out

We fish the shorelines, flats and passes where the schools are moving, and adjust as the bait moves. Some days the action is fast, with fish crashing bait right off the boat. Other days the schools are spread out, and the work is finding where the predators are setting up. You don't need experience, and kids love seeing the bait schools and big fish on the surface.
