**Quick answer:** King mackerel run off Tampa Bay twice a year, peaking in April and May and again in October and November, as the Gulf water passes through the temperatures kings like. The trip built for them is our nearshore charter: 6 hours for $850 or 8 hours for $1,000.

## Why kings show up in spring and fall

Kingfish follow water temperature up and down Florida's Gulf coast. In spring they push north as the Gulf warms, and in fall they head back south as it cools. Twice a year, that carries them right past the mouth of Tampa Bay.

The fall run lines up with a lot of other movement. Bait is on the move, the mullet run is pushing schools toward the Gulf, and the first cool fronts start dropping water temperatures. When the bait and the temperature line up, kings come in close enough to reach without a long offshore run.

## Kingfish or Spanish mackerel?

They share a family name, but they're different trips. Spanish mackerel are smaller and move into the bay itself, which makes for steady action on an inshore trip. Kings are bigger and faster, and they usually stay outside the bay over nearshore structure and open water.

To tell them apart in the boat: Spanish mackerel have bright yellow spots along their sides. Kings don't, and their lateral line drops sharply about halfway down the body.

## What a nearshore king trip looks like

Kings strike fast and run hard. The first run off a good one, with line peeling off the reel, is what people remember. They're also one of several targets on a nearshore day, with snapper, grouper, grunts and sheepshead on the bottom, so a slow king bite doesn't mean a slow day.

The limit is 3 kings per person, at least 24 inches fork length, and there's no closed season. [RECHECK FWC]
