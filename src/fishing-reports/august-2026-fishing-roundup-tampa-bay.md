Eleven oversized trout came over the rail in a row before one finally fit the legal slot. That was a single August morning on Tampa Bay, and the rest of the month was about fish moving into the bay.

## The trout morning

The trout were working the troughs on the incoming tide. As the tide pushed water and bait through those deeper cuts, the bigger fish set up to feed, and once the boat was dialed in, the action never let up.

Big female trout like these are a good sign for the health of the bay. Every oversized one was admired and sent back. Earlier in the month the trout had been holding in the same troughs. See [inshore trips](/inshore/).

## What it said about the bay

**Redfish.** In early August the reds were sticking close to the shoreline and tucking into the mangroves. By mid month their numbers were building throughout the bay as more fish moved in from the surrounding waters. See [redfish trips](/redfish/).

**Sharks.** Shark fishing was good all August, and the smaller sharks were especially active, which meant a lot of shots for the anglers on board. See [shark trips](/shark/).

**Mixed bags.** In the last week of the month, Capt. Pruner and his clients brought a solid mixed bag aboard nearly every day. Rather than lock in on one species, they fished the bay the way it wanted to be fished and took whatever was willing to bite.
