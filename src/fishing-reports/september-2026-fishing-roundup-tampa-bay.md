Gag grouper season opened September 1, and the first two weeks were steady, with the gags eating live pinfish and grunts. That set the tone for a month with something biting on every kind of trip.

## Inside the bay

**Gag grouper:** hot from the opener, on live pinfish and grunts.

**Spanish mackerel:** big schools, with jacks and ladyfish mixed in.

**Mangrove snapper and white grunt:** helping fill the cooler between grouper bites.

**Speckled trout:** a solid, reliable bite.

**Snook:** season open, and the bite was strong.

**Redfish:** schools pushing into the bay in very good numbers, building toward the fall run.

**Sharks:** steady action on the same inshore trips.

## Tripletail on the buoys

The tripletail bite turned on early in the month. They held around crab trap buoys, channel markers and floating debris, sitting up near the surface in the shade. They fight hard all the way to the boat and eat well. To keep one, it has to measure at least 18 inches.

## Offshore when the weather allowed

Whenever conditions let our boats reach the right structure, red grouper and red snapper led the offshore bite. Mangrove, lane and vermilion snapper cooperated too, which kept the fish box full even when one species slowed down. [More on offshore trips](/offshore-fishing/)
