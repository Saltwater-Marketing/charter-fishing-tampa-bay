**Quick answer:** Snook in Tampa Bay can be kept during two windows a year, September 1 through November 30 and March 1 through April 30, one fish per person inside a 28 to 33 inch slot (total length). Outside those windows the season is closed to harvest, but catch and release stays open, and we run snook trips all year. [RECHECK CURRENT FWC RULES BEFORE LAUNCH]

## Gulf rules, not Atlantic rules

Tampa Bay falls under Florida's Gulf snook rules. That matters, because the two coasts have different dates and slot sizes. The closed months line up with when snook need protection most: winter cold is hard on them, and summer is their spawning season.

## Why fall is a good time for snook

As the water cools, snook that spent the summer around the beaches and passes work back into the bay, the rivers and the backcountry. That puts them along the mangroves and docks we fish every day, and the fall mullet run puts a lot of bait in front of them.

## What you can keep

During an open window, the limit is one snook per person, between 28 and 33 inches total length, on hook and line only. Keeping one also takes a snook permit on top of a saltwater license. On our charters, both are covered for everyone on board.

The slot is narrow on purpose. Smaller fish are young, and bigger ones are the breeding females that keep the bay full of snook, so plenty of fish go back even in season.

## A closed season trip

Closed season doesn't mean closed fishing. Every snook goes back, and for most guests that changes nothing, because the fight is the reason to chase them. Quick photos, a wet hand and a fast release, and the fish swims off strong. Redfish and trout share the same flats, so the day rarely slows down.
